# Educational Explainer Skill

## Purpose
Explain a subject accurately and simply without making it simplistic.

## Learning design
Before designing visuals, identify:
- what the audience already knows,
- what they need to learn,
- the misconception or difficulty,
- the single most important takeaway.

Break complex topics into small conceptual steps. Introduce terminology only when needed and explain it in plain language.

## Explanation pattern
A useful default is:
1. familiar hook or question,
2. simple definition,
3. concrete example,
4. visual explanation of how or why it works,
5. recap in plain language.

Adapt this structure to the subject; it is not a rigid formula.

## Visual teaching methods
- Use everyday analogies for abstract concepts.
- Show cause and effect as connected actions.
- Use before/after states for change.
- Use labels and callouts to identify important parts.
- Use scale and comparison to make quantities meaningful.
- Use simple characters or objects when human action helps explain the concept.

## Audience adaptation
For young children:
- use familiar examples from daily life,
- prefer concrete objects over abstract symbols,
- explain one idea at a time,
- use warm, clear language,
- avoid dense labels and fast transitions.

For general audiences:
- explain jargon briefly,
- use examples people can recognize,
- keep the pace comfortable.

## Accuracy and trust
- Separate fact from analogy.
- Do not present a metaphor as literal mechanism.
- Do not invent citations, statistics, or historical details.
- Flag claims that need source verification.
- Preserve important nuance when simplifying.

## Retention
Repeat the central idea in a new form: a visual summary, a short recap, or a final transformation that makes the concept memorable.
