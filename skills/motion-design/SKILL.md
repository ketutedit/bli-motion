# Motion Design Skill

## Purpose
Create a distinct, coherent visual language and purposeful motion system for each project. The goal is not to decorate a layout; it is to make the message visible through design and movement.

## Art direction before animation
Define:
- emotional tone and visual attitude,
- shape vocabulary (geometric, organic, technical, playful, editorial),
- color roles (background, primary, accent, semantic/status),
- typography roles (display, body, labels, numbers),
- illustration and icon treatment,
- material / render family (flat vector, paper, collage, photo, soft 3D, glossy, glass, dimensional UI, sketch),
- texture, lighting, depth, and camera language,
- motion signature and transition logic.

Choose these from the topic, audience, brand assets, and visual concept. Do not default to dark backgrounds, neon glow, particles, giant typography, or 3D just because they are easy to generate.

## Visual system
Establish repeatable tokens for:
- color and contrast,
- type scale and line length,
- spacing and alignment,
- corner radius and stroke weight,
- shadow / depth treatment,
- illustration style,
- easing and motion character.

Keep visual consistency within one video or series. Vary concepts and choreography when creating unrelated videos so they do not look like recolors of one template.

## Composition
- Establish one dominant focal point at a time.
- Use scale, alignment, spacing, contrast, and grouping to create hierarchy.
- Keep labels close to the object or data they describe.
- Use negative space intentionally.
- Keep important content inside platform-safe areas.
- Make every composition work at the final viewing size, especially on mobile.
- Avoid crowding, but do not remove information that is necessary to understand the subject.

## Motion language
Assign motion a communication role:
- **Reveal:** introduce information.
- **Emphasis:** direct attention to a key detail.
- **Transformation:** show a change of state.
- **Connection:** show relationships between elements.
- **Comparison:** make differences visible.
- **Navigation:** move attention through a system or visual space.
- **Atmosphere:** support the mood without competing with information.

Specify each important action as initial state → property/action → final state. Include direction, distance, timing, easing, overlap/stagger, and what remains still.

## Camera and depth
Use camera movement when it contributes to the explanation:
- push in to reveal detail,
- pull back to show context,
- track a moving subject,
- orbit to reveal a system,
- use parallax to separate meaningful layers,
- move through a path to explain sequence or geography.

Do not move the camera merely to make the video appear active. A stable camera is appropriate when object motion already carries the scene.

## Transitions
Prefer transitions motivated by content:
- an element becomes the next scene's element,
- a line grows into a chart or path,
- a shape reveals the next visual,
- a camera move follows a meaningful spatial relation,
- a match cut connects form or action,
- a clean cut marks a real change in topic, time, or viewpoint.

Avoid unrelated transition presets between every scene. A transition should support the edit, not become the subject.

## Background behavior
Decide whether the background should be quiet, textured, layered, atmospheric, or dynamic. Background animation must stay subordinate to the main visual. A scene does not need constant particle motion; deliberate stillness can create emphasis.

## Quality checks
- Is the focal point obvious?
- Does every important movement communicate something?
- Does the visual hierarchy survive at mobile size?
- Are materials, shadows, strokes, and lighting coherent?
- Are the transitions motivated?
- Does the design avoid the accidental slide-deck pattern?
- Can the target renderer reproduce every described effect?
- Have key rendered frames been inspected?
