# Motion Design Skill

## Purpose
Create a coherent visual system and purposeful motion language for a video.

## Visual direction
Define before storyboarding:
- visual tone and emotional temperature,
- shape language (geometric, organic, technical, playful, editorial),
- color roles (background, primary, accent, semantic/status),
- typography roles (display, body, labels, numbers),
- illustration/icon treatment,
- depth model (flat, layered 2.5D, dimensional),
- texture and lighting rules.

Do not choose colors or styles arbitrarily. Tie choices to topic, audience, brand, and legibility.

## Composition
- Establish a clear focal point.
- Use scale, alignment, spacing, contrast, and grouping to create hierarchy.
- Keep important content inside platform-safe areas.
- Avoid crowding; allow breathing room.
- Make the composition work at the final viewing size, especially on mobile.

## Motion language
Assign motion roles:
- **Reveal:** introduce information.
- **Emphasis:** direct attention to a key detail.
- **Transformation:** show a change of state.
- **Connection:** show relationships between elements.
- **Comparison:** make differences visible.
- **Navigation:** move attention between sections.
- **Atmosphere:** subtle supporting movement only.

Atmospheric motion must never compete with information.

## Transitions
Prefer transitions motivated by content:
- an element becomes the next scene's element,
- a line grows into a chart or path,
- a shape masks/reveals the next idea,
- camera or layout movement follows a meaningful spatial relationship.

Avoid unrelated transition presets between every scene.

## Consistency
Maintain consistent:
- corner radius and stroke logic,
- shadow/depth treatment,
- spacing scale,
- typography scale,
- easing family,
- icon/illustration style.

## Production test
Ask whether every described element and movement can be built in the target engine. If not, simplify or specify a fallback.
