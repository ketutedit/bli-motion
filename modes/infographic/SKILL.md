# Infographic & Data Motion Mode

## Load when

The task visualizes data, quantities, comparisons, systems, hierarchies, geography, timelines, or structured information.

## Choose the visual form from the data

- comparison: aligned bars, paired marks, or comparable objects;
- trend: line chart or sequential states;
- part-to-whole: stacked or proportional forms when the relationship is valid;
- process: connected steps or a path;
- hierarchy: tree, nested structure, or grouped levels;
- system: labeled diagram with meaningful connections;
- geography: map only when location is relevant;
- quantity: repeated units or a chart with an explicit scale.

Do not select a chart because it looks attractive.

## Data integrity

Preserve units, scale, baseline, labels, source, timeframe, and uncertainty. Do not invent or distort numbers. Distinguish measured values, estimates, projections, and illustrative examples.

When no trustworthy data is available, create a conceptual diagram instead of fabricating a numerical chart.

## Animate the insight

Establish what is being measured, reveal the relevant scale and categories, animate values or relationships, and emphasize the supported takeaway. Leave the completed visualization visible long enough to understand.

Use annotations close to the data they explain. Do not make a viewer decode a complicated legend when direct labels would be clearer.

## Visual hierarchy and access

Use color with labels, position, patterns, or shape so meaning does not depend on color alone. Check contrast, text size, number formatting, and small-screen legibility.

## Quality check

Is the data truthful? Is the chart type appropriate? Can the viewer see the main insight without guessing? Does the animation preserve rather than distort the information?
