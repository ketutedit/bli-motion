# Character & 2D/3D Motion Mode

## Load when

A character or expressive object carries the action, emotion, explanation, or narrative. Use for 2D, 3D, cutout, vector, rigged, or procedural character work.

## Establish the character

Define only what is needed for the task:

- silhouette and proportion;
- personality and emotional range;
- shape language and visual treatment;
- pose and gesture vocabulary;
- movement ability and physical limits;
- camera, scale, and environment;
- continuity rules across shots.

Keep character design consistent across views and scenes. Do not introduce unnecessary detail that weakens the silhouette.

## Acting and staging

Make the intention readable through pose, gaze, timing, and gesture. Stage the primary action clearly before adding secondary motion.

Use anticipation, weight shift, contact, follow-through, and holds to communicate intent. A character should not continuously bounce or blink without purpose.

## Weight and motion

Relate movement to mass, balance, momentum, contact, and emotional state. Feet, hands, props, and body weight should interact plausibly with the environment. Exaggeration is useful when it improves readability and suits the style.

## 2D and 3D choices

Use vector or cutout techniques for graphic clarity and efficient iteration. Use 3D when volume, lighting, camera, spatial interaction, or dimensional performance materially benefits the idea.

Choose a renderer and rigging approach based on performance, editability, output quality, and available assets. Do not claim complex character rigging or simulation is available unless the implementation supports it.

## Continuity and verification

Check proportions, facing direction, screen position, props, lighting, and character identity across cuts. Inspect poses at key frames and review the full action for popping, sliding, penetration, clipping, or inconsistent contact.

## Quality check

Can the audience read the action and emotion? Is the silhouette clear? Does the character have weight and continuity? Does secondary motion support the performance?
