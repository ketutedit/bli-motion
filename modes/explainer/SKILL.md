# Explainer & Visual Storytelling Mode

## Load when

The viewer must understand a subject, mechanism, process, relationship, comparison, timeline, or evidence-based story. Do not load for a simple logo reveal, isolated UI interaction, or abstract loop unless explanation is also a goal.

## Creative decisions

- Identify the one or two ideas the audience must understand.
- Identify prior knowledge, likely misconceptions, evidence, and terms that need explanation.
- Choose a structure appropriate to the subject: process, cause/effect, system, comparison, scale, timeline, question/answer, or character-led explanation.
- Choose a visual device that reveals how the idea works.
- Keep factual claims, sourced data, metaphor, illustration, and reconstruction distinct.

## Visual storytelling

Show relationships, not just nouns. Use transformations, paths, cutaways, scale changes, connected objects, layered systems, or character actions when they clarify the topic.

The image should add information beyond the narration. Avoid simply placing a still illustration beside a paragraph and changing the slide for every sentence.

A scene may develop through several visual beats. A video does not need a conventional hook/context/explanation/recap structure when another sequence communicates better.

## Pacing and text

- Introduce unfamiliar concepts one at a time.
- Keep on-screen copy concise but preserve information necessary for understanding.
- Hold important terms, diagrams, and conclusions long enough to read.
- Adapt density and vocabulary to the audience and screen size.
- Use a recap or payoff only when it strengthens understanding.

## Accuracy

Do not invent figures, quotes, dates, causal relationships, maps, or sources. Preserve units, context, and uncertainty. Label illustrative metaphors where viewers could mistake them for literal mechanisms or documentary evidence.

## Quality check

Can the viewer explain the central relationship after watching? Does the visual demonstrate it? Is the metaphor accurate enough? Is the conclusion supported by the evidence?
