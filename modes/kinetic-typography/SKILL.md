# Kinetic Typography & Editorial Mode

## Load when

Typography is the primary visual subject or the piece depends on animated titles, words, numbers, captions, editorial layouts, or type-led transitions.

## Treat type as language and form

Determine the role of each text element: headline, key phrase, body copy, label, number, caption, or decorative type. Use hierarchy, alignment, scale, weight, contrast, and line breaks to establish reading order.

The typography should express the tone and meaning of the words, not merely animate because it can.

## Motion choices

Choose movement according to language and emphasis:

- reveal or mask to control information;
- tracking or weight change to express tension or expansion;
- scale and position to establish importance;
- word/line sequencing to follow spoken emphasis;
- rotation, distortion, or deformation only when legibility and intent support it;
- morphing or match cuts to connect related words and forms.

Do not scatter letters, rotate every word, or use per-character animation by default.

## Readability and pacing

- Preserve correct spelling, punctuation, and language-specific shaping.
- Set intentional line breaks and safe margins.
- Keep text stable long enough to read.
- Avoid excessive movement during reading.
- Check font availability, fallback, kerning, and text measurement in the target renderer.
- Treat captions and subtitles as a readability task, not merely a decorative animation.

## Editorial composition

Use grids, asymmetric balance, cropping, scale contrast, negative space, imagery, and pace according to the concept. Do not default to centered giant text on a gradient background.

## Quality check

Can the text be read at the intended size? Does motion add emphasis or meaning? Is the hierarchy clear? Does the typography feel authored rather than automatically animated?
