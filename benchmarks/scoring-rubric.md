# BLI Motion Scoring Rubric

Use this rubric for blind comparisons. Score each dimension from 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent). Include a short evidence note for every score.

| Dimension | 1 — Weak | 3 — Competent | 5 — Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brief fit | Misses key intent or constraints | Meets the main request | Meets intent and handles constraints elegantly |
| Creative specificity | Generic, interchangeable treatment | Some choices fit the subject | Distinct visual idea that feels purpose-built |
| Art direction | Inconsistent or poorly resolved | Coherent and readable | Highly coherent, intentional, and polished |
| Composition | Weak hierarchy or clutter | Clear hierarchy | Excellent control of focus, space, and visual balance |
| Motion quality | Mechanical, random, or distracting | Mostly purposeful and controlled | Precise, expressive, and tightly choreographed |
| Communication | Key idea is unclear | Main idea is understandable | Visuals make the idea immediately and memorably clear |
| Continuity and timing | Abrupt, confusing, or rushed | Generally coherent | Rhythm and transitions strengthen the experience |
| Technical reliability | Broken, missing, or visibly defective | Works with minor issues | Stable, clean, reproducible output |
| Efficiency | Excessive complexity or rework | Reasonable effort | Strong result with proportionate production effort |

## Critical-failure flags

Mark separately when any of the following occurs:

- incorrect facts or misleading visualization;
- text that cannot be read at delivery size;
- missing, broken, or substituted assets;
- major clipping or overlap;
- render failure or corrupt export;
- broken loop where seamless looping was required;
- user constraints or supplied brand assets ignored.

## Interpretation

Compare per-dimension results, critical failures, and reviewer notes. Do not rely on a single average. A skill can improve reliability while reducing originality, or improve aesthetics while making production substantially slower. Report those trade-offs.
