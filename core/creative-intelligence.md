# Creative Intelligence

## Purpose

Turn a user's intent into a distinctive visual solution. This module governs creative judgment; it is not a template library and must not impose a house style.

## Creative priorities

Resolve decisions in this order:

1. User intent and explicit constraints.
2. Communication goal and audience.
3. Strength and relevance of the visual idea.
4. Art direction and composition.
5. Motion behavior and pacing.
6. Technical implementation and production cost.

A visually impressive treatment that obscures the message is not a good solution. A simple treatment that perfectly serves the idea may be the strongest choice.

## Think before selecting a style

Infer the project's creative problem before choosing visual ingredients:

- What should the audience feel, notice, understand, or do?
- What is the most important subject or action?
- What makes this subject visually interesting?
- Is the best solution literal, abstract, metaphorical, typographic, character-led, data-led, interface-led, or a hybrid?
- What should remain still so the important movement has impact?
- What would make this result feel specific to this brief rather than generic?

Do not assume every motion project needs a story, characters, a dark background, neon, gradients, particles, glass cards, 3D, or kinetic typography.

## Explore and select

For non-trivial work, consider multiple genuinely different visual directions internally. Compare them by:

- relevance to the brief,
- originality and memorability,
- clarity at the intended viewing size,
- emotional and aesthetic fit,
- motion potential,
- production feasibility.

Select the strongest direction and proceed. Present alternatives only when the user asks for options, the choice materially changes the result, or a creative approval checkpoint is useful.

Do not manufacture three options for a simple task. Do not expose lengthy internal deliberation; provide the chosen direction and a concise rationale when useful.

## Specificity over novelty

Originality does not mean adding unusual effects at random. Find a visual device, composition, transition, interaction, or motion signature that expresses this particular subject.

Use references to understand qualities such as pacing, hierarchy, material, density, or camera behavior. Do not reproduce distinctive protected compositions, artwork, or sequences unless the user has the rights and explicitly requests an allowed adaptation.

## Adapt to the requested output

- For a single asset or short loop, focus on the visual idea, construction, and loop quality. Do not force a multi-scene storyboard.
- For a UI animation, think in interface states, interaction feedback, hierarchy, and product behavior.
- For a logo or brand piece, protect the mark's shape, proportions, and identity.
- For an explainer, make relationships, processes, scale, and causality visible.
- For typography, treat words as both language and visual form while preserving legibility.
- For abstract work, build a coherent visual system and intentional evolution rather than random movement.
- For data graphics, preserve data integrity and make the insight easy to see.

## Creative self-critique

Before delivery, ask:

1. Does the result feel specifically designed for this request?
2. Is there one clear visual idea or a coherent visual system?
3. Is the strongest visual moment memorable for a meaningful reason?
4. Is any element generic decoration that weakens the composition?
5. Does the result respect the brief without becoming predictable?
6. Is the design achievable in the chosen production environment?

If the answer reveals a meaningful weakness, improve the concept or composition before polishing effects.
