# Visual Quality and Verification

## Purpose

Evaluate the actual deliverable, not the confidence of the description or the elegance of the source code.

## Verification levels

Report only the level actually completed:

1. **Reviewed concept** — creative direction or storyboard reviewed.
2. **Reviewed source** — code and assets inspected statically.
3. **Built** — the project completed its build or type check.
4. **Previewed** — an actual preview or rendered frames were viewed.
5. **Rendered** — the requested output file was produced.
6. **Playback checked** — the exported media was played or inspected over time.
7. **Final verified** — visual, technical, and delivery requirements were checked.

A successful build does not prove visual quality. A still frame does not prove the full animation or audio sync.

## Visual critique

Inspect the most informative moments for the project:

- opening or first state;
- first appearance of the main subject;
- key transformation or interaction;
- densest composition;
- important transition;
- climax or signature moment;
- ending and loop boundary where applicable.

For multi-scene work, sample enough frames to judge continuity and pacing. Use full playback when timing, interaction, or audio behavior cannot be judged from stills.

## Evaluation dimensions

Score each dimension from 1 to 5 when a structured review is useful:

| Dimension | Review question |
|---|---|
| Intent fit | Does the output solve the actual brief? |
| Creative specificity | Does it feel designed for this subject rather than generic? |
| Art direction | Are composition, color, type, material, and image treatment coherent? |
| Hierarchy | Is the viewer's attention guided clearly? |
| Motion quality | Are timing, spacing, choreography, and transitions intentional? |
| Readability | Can key information be understood at delivery size? |
| Continuity | Do visual states and scenes connect appropriately? |
| Technical integrity | Are there clipping, overlap, missing assets, artifacts, or timing errors? |

Do not treat the numerical average as a substitute for judgment. A critical failure in legibility, factual accuracy, or technical integrity must be fixed even if the average score is high.

## Prioritize fixes

Fix issues in this order:

1. wrong or unclear message;
2. weak or generic concept;
3. composition and hierarchy;
4. continuity and choreography;
5. timing and readability;
6. technical defects;
7. decorative polish.

Do not polish effects while a fundamental creative problem remains.

## Iteration discipline

Make targeted changes based on observed problems. Avoid endless random redesigns.

After a significant change, inspect the affected frames or states again. Stop when the requirements are met and further changes are unlikely to materially improve the result.

## Honest reporting

State which verification levels were completed and identify remaining limitations. Never claim to have watched, rendered, tested, or inspected assets that were not actually available.
