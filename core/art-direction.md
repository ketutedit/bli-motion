# Art Direction System

## Purpose

Create a visual identity that belongs to the brief. These guidelines define decision quality, not a mandatory BLI Motion house style.

## Build the visual language

Select only the dimensions that matter to the project:

- mood and emotional attitude;
- composition and visual hierarchy;
- shape vocabulary;
- color roles and contrast;
- typography and text behavior;
- image, illustration, and icon treatment;
- material, texture, lighting, and depth;
- camera perspective and movement;
- transitions and motion signature;
- sound relationship when relevant.

Explain important choices through their relationship to the subject, audience, brand, or intended feeling.

## Avoid default-style bias

There is no required default palette, background, font, card treatment, material, or transition.

Do not reflexively choose:

- dark navy backgrounds;
- cyan/purple neon accents;
- gradient glow;
- glassmorphism;
- floating cards;
- particles and grids;
- oversized centered headlines;
- 3D objects;
- spring/bounce entrances;
- generic fade/scale transitions.

These techniques are valid when they serve the selected direction. They are not quality markers by themselves.

## Establish a coherent system

For a project, define reusable tokens only where consistency is needed:

- color roles rather than an arbitrary list of hex values;
- typography roles and readable size relationships;
- spacing and alignment logic;
- stroke, corner, shadow, and depth conventions;
- image and illustration treatment;
- motion durations, easing character, and stagger logic.

Consistency does not mean repetition. Vary layout, framing, rhythm, and visual events while preserving the identity.

## Composition and hierarchy

- Give the viewer a clear first point of attention.
- Use scale, position, contrast, grouping, and negative space intentionally.
- Keep related labels close to the object they describe.
- Avoid filling empty space without a communication reason.
- Check the design at its actual output size and platform crop.
- Use asymmetry, overlap, depth, and off-center composition when they improve the idea.

## Typography

Treat typography as both information and visual material. Choose type behavior according to tone and readability.

Do not animate every word or character by default. Preserve line breaks, spacing, contrast, and enough reading time. Verify font availability and fallback behavior in the target renderer.

## Material and dimensionality

Choose flat vector, editorial photography, collage, paper, dimensional UI, soft 3D, hard-surface 3D, sketch, or mixed media according to the subject. Use lighting, shadow, texture, blur, and depth consistently within a scene.

Do not simulate depth with excessive glow or shadows when clean flat design is stronger.

## Reference interpretation

When a reference is supplied, identify the qualities the user values: hierarchy, pace, color relationship, material, camera, density, or transition behavior.

Create a solution that serves the same intent without copying distinctive protected artwork or an exact composition unless the user owns it and requests that adaptation.

## Final direction check

Ask whether the visual language is:
- specific to the brief;
- coherent across the piece;
- readable at delivery size;
- distinct from generic AI design habits;
- technically reproducible.

Revise the art direction when it relies on a pile of effects instead of a clear visual idea.
